Guide: Health

BMI explained

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple number that compares weight to height. It is a quick screening tool, not a diagnosis.

The formula

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres)2

For example, a person weighing 65 kg who is 1.70 m tall has a BMI of about 22.5 (65 ÷ 2.89).

WHO categories for adults

Below 18.5 is underweight. From 18.5 to 24.9 is the normal range. From 25.0 to 29.9 is overweight. 30.0 and above falls in the obese range.

Where BMI falls short

BMI does not measure body fat directly. Muscular people can have a high BMI without excess fat. It also does not show where fat is stored. Older adults, children and some ethnic groups may need different interpretation. Waist size is a useful companion measure.

What to do with your result

Use BMI as a starting point for a conversation with a doctor or dietitian. If your result is outside the normal range, or if you have concerns about your weight or eating, speak to a qualified health professional rather than making drastic changes on your own.
