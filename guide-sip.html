Guide: Investing

How SIP works

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually every month, into a mutual fund. Small regular amounts make it easier to start, and the habit matters as much as the amount.

Why compounding matters

When your returns earn returns of their own, your money grows faster over time. This is compounding. The longer the period, the stronger the effect, which is why starting early usually helps more than investing a larger amount later.

How the estimate is calculated

Our SIP calculator uses the monthly rate (annual expected return divided by 12) and assumes each instalment is invested at the start of the month. The estimated value is:

Future value = P × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)

Here P is the monthly amount, i is the monthly return and n is the number of months.

A worked example

If you invest ₹5,000 every month for 10 years and the assumed return is 12% a year, you invest ₹6,00,000 in total. The estimated value is about ₹11,60,000, which means an estimated gain of about ₹5,60,000.

Important limits

Mutual fund returns are not guaranteed. Markets can rise and fall, and a fixed 12% a year is only an assumption for illustration. Past performance does not predict future results. Check the fund factsheet, its risk level, and the exit load before investing.

This guide explains the general method and is not investment advice. If you are unsure, speak to a SEBI registered adviser.
