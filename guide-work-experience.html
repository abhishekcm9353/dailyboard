Calculating work experience for a resume

Employers look at your work experience closely, so the numbers need to be accurate. Counting it correctly is simple once you know the method.

Count from the joining date

Start from your joining date and end on your last working day. If you still work there, use today's date. Count complete months, then convert the months into years. For example, from 1 June 2022 to 15 November 2026 is 4 years, 5 months and 14 days.

Show dates clearly

Write each job with its month and year, such as "Jun 2022 to Nov 2026 (4 years 5 months)". This lets a recruiter check the figure at a glance.

Handle gaps honestly

If you took a break between jobs, show the gap. Employers understand breaks for study, family or health. Hiding a gap, or stretching a job's dates, can cause problems at background verification.

Total experience versus relevant experience

Total experience counts all paid work. Relevant experience counts only work related to the job you are applying for. If you have both, list the relevant roles first and mention the total in your summary.

Keep proof ready

Keep offer letters, relevant experience letters and payslips. Background checks often ask for them, and they make your claims easy to confirm.
